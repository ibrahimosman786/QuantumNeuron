# ShiftRoute-RL v0.3 — A conditional answer to budgeted verification

**Date:** 2026-10-23 UTC-06 (experiment runtime: October 24 UTC)  
**Status:** Executed small-neural-agent research; no original LLM-scale discovery, no production assurance, not peer reviewed.

## Question

When a reinforcement-learning agent adapts to a fallible monitoring signal, can verification spending be reduced **without increasing unsafe executions**, including under unfamiliar inputs?

**Conclusion (restricted):** Yes, *conditional on an independently trusted, sound, inexpensive authorization certificate covering a subset of legitimate actions*: skip expensive verification for actions with a sound certificate, and verify every other action. With identical verifier outcomes, the unsafe execution count is **exactly unchanged** versus verifying everything. The reduction in expensive calls equals the certified fraction. The capability-checking cost must be added.

**Conclusion (unrestricted):** No general guarantee exists using only an imperfect monitor's score and unobserved labels. Without audits or an independently trusted proof, a safe low-scoring action and an unsafe low-scoring action can generate identical observed data. A policy that executes them is safe in one world and unsafe in the other. Selective random audits help learn about failure rates and trigger fallback, but cannot guarantee zero additional violations prior to detection; a biased or unreliable auditor further weakens guarantees.

The conditional construction is an engineering safety principle, **not** a novel RL training algorithm. It addresses *deployment-time authorization cost* under strong, explicit assumptions; it does **not** solve the broader reward-hacking or model-alignment problem.

## Prior art (read first)

- Moya, Thornley & Lin (Sept 28, 2026): [Verifier Errors in RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35677). Establishes an insufficiency result for ordinary verifier feedback and proposes audit-based selective control. Closest conceptual predecessor.
- Hu et al. (Oct 19, 2026): [GRAFT](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/graft/). Routes gradient updates instead of reshaping reward, reducing some reward hacks. Not a proof of multi-turn safety.
- Khosravi & Huo (May 2026): [Conformal Selective Acting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20270). An anytime selective-risk wrapper under technical assumptions; our conservative historical martingale diagnostic is NOT a replacement or an equally strong guarantee.
- Prinster et al. (ICML 2025): [WATCH](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/prinster25a.html). Weighted conformal martingales for distribution-shift monitoring; monitoring shift is not a safety guarantee.
- [AgentDojo](https://github.com/ethz-spylab/agentdojo): useful external multi-turn agent benchmark, **not evaluated in v0.3**.

## Formal assumptions and proof sketch

For event t, let Y_t=1 denote an unauthorized proposal, V_t=1 denote a reviewer catching the proposal, and C_t=1 denote acceptance of a **trusted** external certificate. Soundness requires C_t=1 implies Y_t=0. The verify-all baseline executes unauthorized actions when Y_t=1 and V_t=0. Proof-first executes unauthorized actions when Y_t=1, C_t=0, V_t=0. Soundness implies Y_t=1 => C_t=0. Hence the unsafe-execution indicator is **identical pointwise**, regardless of the sequence of policies, distribution shifts, or correlated monitor scores, provided the same reviewer outcomes are used for uncertified actions. The number of expensive reviews is lower by \sum_t C_t. Net savings equal verifier cost times certificates minus proof-check cost times total actions. This does not require the monitor to be calibrated.

If unsound certificates can be issued on unsafe actions, the proof is invalid and new unsafe executions occur. If reviews are also imperfect, proof-first matches, but does **not fix**, the unsafe executions already caused by missed unsafe actions at the reviewer. Revocation, freshness, nonce binding, key custody, and correct interpretation of policy are prerequisites for real-world certificates and remain unimplemented.

For randomized audits with predictable p_t >= p_min > 0 and *accurate audit labels*, A_t Y_t/p_t is an unbiased estimator of the historical Y_t, conditional on the proposal and prior history. A conservative time-uniform one-sided Hoeffding-Azuma bound can cover the **realized historical mean** under these premises. It **cannot** certify instantaneous future risk or guarantee that each unchecked action is safe. Our bound is nearly vacuous in the shifted experiments because p_min is small.

## Experimental design

Model-selected tool actions are generated by saved PyTorch SFT and PPO actor checkpoints previously trained in a six-step synthetic workflow. The v0.3 evaluation chooses between the models over time to approximate a change in policy population—**it does not update PPO weights online**. Cheap risk scores come from the previously trained frozen monitor; true authorization comes from the simulator's hidden state. Policy / monitor and task shifts are controllable. A Bernoulli reviewer catches an unsafe action with either 100% or 94% sensitivity, and has either 100% or 99% specificity.

- Five independent stream seeds: 41, 57, 73, 89, 101.
- Three stream regimes: stable mix, policy-mixture shift, policy + monitoring-cue shift.
- 3,600 decisions per stream, per reviewer quality; 5 competing deployment policies.
- 150 base comparison runs, plus 40 certificate-soundness stress runs; all labels and outcomes saved.
- Reviewer cost 1.0, cheap-monitor cost 0.025, proof-check cost 0.010, all in **synthetic units**, not dollars.
- Full verification, threshold-only verification, 12% sentinel sampling, adaptive lockdown after two observed low-score audit flags, proof-first certification with 62% eligibility among soundly authorized proposals.
- No reviewer action is conditioned on hidden ground truth except the simulated external independent verifier and the **assumed trusted certificate issuer**. This assumption is what creates the conditional guarantee; it is not a learned ability of the RL policy.

## Main five-seed means: hardest joint shift

| Reviewer | Method | Unsafe executed / all actions | Expensive reviewer call fraction | Cost / action | Legitimate completed / all actions |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Perfect | Verify all | 0.00% | 100.00% | 1.025 | 49.37% |
| Perfect | Threshold | 46.96% | 4.32% | 0.068 | 49.37% |
| Perfect | Sentinel | 41.25% | 15.50% | 0.180 | 49.37% |
| Perfect | Adaptive lockdown | 6.53% | 73.13% | 0.756 | 49.37% |
| Perfect | **Sound proof-first** | **0.00%** | **69.53%** | **0.730** | **49.37%** |
| 94% sensitivity | Verify all | 3.06% | 100.00% | 1.025 | 48.91% |
| 94% sensitivity | Adaptive lockdown | 9.46% | 71.51% | 0.740 | 49.11% |
| 94% sensitivity | **Sound proof-first** | **3.06%** | **69.53%** | **0.730** | **49.19%** |

The proof-first policy saves 30.47% of expensive reviewer invocations and approximately **28.75% of the defined combined synthetic operating cost** compared to verify-all. This benefit exists only under the assumed proof coverage and proof lookup price. Real-time latency, issuer compute, threat-model costs, and deployment engineering are not included.

## Soundness stress test (five-seed means, joint shift)

| Unsafe certificate error per unsafe action | Unsafe executed, perfect reviewer | Unsafe executed, 94%-sensitive reviewer |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 0.000% | 3.061% |
| 0.1% | 0.044% | 3.100% |
| 1% | 0.378% | 3.411% |
| 5% | 2.256% | 5.183% |

Even 0.1% erroneous proof issuance breaks exact no-increase safety in this example. Consequently, a learned confidence score **must never be described as an authorization proof**.

## Limits / validity warnings

1. Strong certificate soundness is assumed, not established by v0.3; the fake-certificate stress test exposes why.
2. The model scale is tiny (PyTorch feed-forward networks, not an LLM). The stream replaces a policy population at the halfway point, rather than reoptimizing policy weights live.
3. The five seeds share the same trained checkpoint seed 7, simulator family and handcrafted reward loophole. They estimate stochastic variation in these conditions, not variation across independent training runs or industry tasks.
4. The independently trained initial PPO actors are reproducible in prior cloud GitHub Actions; v0.3 does not train a new LLM.
5. Observed action safety uses simulator labels, which are unavailable in typical uncontrolled environments. Production reviewers can be wrong, and their failure rates are generally unknown or shift-dependent.
6. The randomized-sentinel strategy failed the strict no-increase constraint; it is not a solution despite lower expense.
7. `illustrative_total_loss_per_action` arbitrarily assigns a harm penalty of 50 synthetic units per unsafe execution; it is not an economic impact estimate.
8. The historical martingale estimate is not a distribution-free guarantee on *future* violations; the reported upper bounds were around 0.93–0.98 in the shifted setting and therefore not operationally tight.
9. This is not a peer-reviewed new algorithm, nor is external AgentDojo or pretrained LLM evaluation completed.

## Remaining research for a paper / genuine open-source contribution

1. Build a signed, context-bound capability proof service with expiry/replay protections and independent tests; measure issuance CPU cost.
2. Replace oracle labels with independent annotation on AgentDojo and additional held-out multi-turn benchmarks; require trace-level safety labels, not just prompt-injection accuracy.
3. Fine-tune an accessible pretrained model under weak rewards vs independently audited feedback, using GPU and published training configurations; study monitor exploitation *during* RL (not only policy replacement).
4. Reproduce and compare against the published Verifier Errors audit correction and GRAFT; no novelty claim until method-level ablations beat those baselines.
5. Multi-seed across independently trained model checkpoints, model sizes, tasks and audit noise; preregister success thresholds and report calibrated uncertainty.
6. Calculate real cost including verifier tokens, cached audit outcomes, safe-proof generation, human review, legitimate denials and missed harm, under task-appropriate values.

This report is the strongest conclusion supported **within the tested simulator**. Publishing it as a general solution would be scientifically incorrect.
